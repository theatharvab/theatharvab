Machine-learning-assisted regenerative cooling channel optimization

A computational study exploring whether a neural-network surrogate can accelerate the screening of rectangular cooling-channel designs for liquid rocket engines, with a selected design compared against OpenFOAM CFD.

Research question

Can a neural-network surrogate trained on a physics-generated dataset help screen regenerative cooling channel geometries more quickly than running a high-resolution CFD simulation for every candidate, while producing predictions consistent with CFD for an evaluated design?

The project focused on rectangular channel geometry and thermal-fluid constraints. It was a computational design-space study, not a combustion simulation or an experimental engine test.

Method

  • Generated a physics-based dataset and trained a PyTorch neural network to predict thermal performance and wall temperature from channel geometry.
  • Used Monte Carlo sampling to screen 20,000 candidate geometries. After a geometric-height filter, 12,699 remained; 1,498 satisfied all implemented constraints.
  • Applied hard limits including a maximum wall temperature of 825 K and a maximum pressure drop of 1.8 MPa, alongside coolant velocity, Reynolds number, outlet temperature, and geometry constraints.
  • Ran OpenFOAM 14 using a k-epsilon turbulence model in WSL Ubuntu, then inspected temperature distributions in ParaView.
  • Compared three mesh sizes: 38,400, 307,200, and 2,457,600 cells.

For the selected comparison, the physics-based model predicted 564.19 K, approximately 1.99% above the fine-mesh CFD result. A single surrogate evaluation took approximately 0.000382 seconds versus 8,206.8 seconds for the fine-mesh CFD evaluation in the recorded setup.

The reported speed ratio compares one surrogate evaluation with one fine-mesh CFD evaluation. It excludes model training and setup time and should not be interpreted as an end-to-end project speedup.

Limitations & scope

  • Gas-side heat transfer was prescribed; combustion was not simulated.
  • Liquid methane coolant was modeled using constant properties.
  • Validation was computational rather than experimental, and mesh independence was not fully established.
  • Generalization beyond the sampled design space is limited and requires additional validation.
  • The model uses physics-generated data and constraint screening; it is not a PDE-residual physics-informed neural network (PINN).

A throat-region stress test produced CFD temperatures near 1,475 K, well above the 825 K constraint. This illustrates why designs outside the modeled training range cannot be trusted without further analysis.

Next steps

Future work could include broader validation across the design space, stronger mesh-independence studies, and comparisons against experimentally sourced or independently validated cases. Extending the workflow to additional thermal-fluid and high-speed-flow problems is a longer-term goal.

Research status: Presented at the 2026 Texas Junior Academy of Science, Engineering I, and received Honorable Mention. The paper has not been published or peer-reviewed.
